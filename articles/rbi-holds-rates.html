RBI holds the repo rate at 5.25% — reading the neutral stance

After three consecutive cuts earlier in the cycle, the Monetary Policy Committee has now paused for a third straight review. Here's what it's waiting on.

By Ishan Gautam·Monetary Policy·Updated September 2026

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee has kept the repo rate unchanged at 5.25% in its most recent reviews, maintaining the "neutral" stance it has held since easing began to slow earlier this year. The Standing Deposit Facility rate stays at 5.00%, while the Marginal Standing Facility and Bank Rate both remain at 5.50% — the corridor around the repo rate that hasn't moved through the middle of FY27.

To put the pause in context: between February and June of 2025, the RBI cut rates three times in a row, taking the repo rate down by a cumulative 125 basis points from 6.50%. Since then, the committee has held steady through multiple reviews. That's a deliberate shift from an easing cycle to a wait-and-watch posture, and it's worth understanding why.

What the MPC is balancing

Two things are pulling in opposite directions. On one side, growth has been strong — India's GDP has repeatedly beaten the RBI's own projections through FY26 and into FY27, which reduces the urgency to cut further just to support activity. On the other side, retail inflation has been drifting back up toward and above the RBI's 4% target after a period of comfortable readings, which argues against cutting rates and adding more demand into an economy where prices are already firming.

A neutral stance is the RBI's way of saying it isn't committed to a direction — the next move could be a cut or a hold, and it will be shaped almost entirely by how the next two or three inflation prints come in.

The external backdrop

The committee's recent reviews have also had to factor in developments beyond India's borders — tariff actions from the United States, global capital flow volatility, and the currency's performance. The RBI has paired its rate decisions with a set of supporting measures aimed at attracting foreign capital and stabilising the rupee, including extended concessional forex swap windows and hedging cost support for banks raising foreign currency deposits — moves that work alongside the interest rate decision rather than through it.

What it means for borrowers and savers

For anyone with a floating-rate home loan, a held repo rate means EMIs stay where they are for now — no fresh relief, but no fresh pain either. For savers, deposit rates that had begun drifting down through the cutting cycle should stay roughly where they are until the RBI signals a clearer direction. The next meaningful trigger to watch is the inflation trajectory: if price pressures ease back toward 4%, a cut becomes plausible again; if they keep climbing, the neutral stance could tilt toward caution rather than easing.
