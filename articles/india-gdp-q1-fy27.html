Growth

India's economy grew 7.8% in Q1 FY27 — what's actually behind the number

The April–June quarter beat every major forecast. Here's where the growth came from, and why one strong print doesn't settle the debate on the rest of FY27.

By Ishan Gautam·Economy·Updated September 2026

India's economy expanded 7.8% year-on-year in the April–June quarter of FY27, comfortably ahead of the 7.1% economists had expected in a Reuters poll and the 7.3% median forecast from a separate Moneycontrol survey. Real GDP came in at ₹81.36 lakh crore against ₹75.46 lakh crore a year earlier, while nominal GDP — which folds in inflation — rose a sharper 10.3% to ₹88.27 lakh crore, according to data from the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation.

12.1%Growth in financial, real estate & IT services

The headline number is only half the story. Gross Value Added, which strips out taxes and subsidies to show underlying activity, grew 8.2% for the quarter. That's a better print than a number inflated by one or two temporary factors — the growth shows up broadly across sectors rather than in a single spike.

Services carried the quarter

The tertiary sector expanded 10% overall, and within it, financial services, real estate, IT and professional services grew 12.1% — the standout performer of the quarter. That's consistent with what the sector has delivered through much of FY26 and FY27 so far: India's services engine, particularly financial and IT-adjacent services, remains the most reliable growth driver in the economy right now.

Manufacturing and construction weren't far behind. The secondary sector — manufacturing, construction and related activity — grew 8.6%, a healthy number that suggests capital expenditure and industrial activity are holding up better than the tariff-related anxiety of the past year would have suggested.

Growth was lower than the revised 8.6% recorded in Q4 FY26 — a moderation, but one that needs to be read in the context of an unusually strong base year, not as a slowdown.

Where it was softer

The primary sector — agriculture and allied activities — grew just 2.9%, with agriculture itself up 3.6%. That's the weakest link in an otherwise strong quarter, and it's the number worth watching heading into the second half of the fiscal year, particularly given how sensitive rural demand and rural inflation are to farm output.

Why this matters for the rest of FY27

Sequentially, growth did moderate from the revised 8.6% clocked in the January–March FY26 quarter. That's not unusual — Q4 numbers tend to run hot — but it's a reminder that the RBI's own projections for the rest of FY27 assume some cooling through the year. The central bank had already trimmed its FY27 growth forecast to 6.6% in its June review, citing global uncertainty, so a 7.8% opening quarter gives the economy real headroom before it needs to worry about undershooting that number.

The more useful question isn't whether 7.8% is a good number — it clearly is — but whether the composition holds. Services-led growth is good news for jobs in finance, IT and real estate-adjacent sectors, but a durable expansion still needs agriculture and rural demand to come along, and that's the piece this quarter didn't deliver.
